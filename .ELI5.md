# Signal Board — explained like you're 5

## What is this thing?

It's a website that shows you a list of really good TV shows and movies —
like a leaderboard for "stuff worth watching."

## How does it know what's good?

It uses [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com) — a giant website where millions of
people rate every movie and show ever made, from 1 star to 10 stars. Signal
Board grabs a snapshot of all those ratings once in a while and keeps a copy
of it, so it doesn't need to ask IMDb every single time someone visits.

To stop one weird show that only 12 people rated 10/10 from sneaking to the
top, it only counts shows/films with a decent number of votes — enough
people had to have watched and rated it for it to count.

## What do the buttons do?

- **TV shows / Films** — pick which kind of thing you want ranked.
- **Rating / Popular** — "Rating" is the best-of-all-time list. "Popular"
  is more like "what's getting watched a lot lately" — it looks at recent
  releases and ranks them by how many people have voted on them.
- **This year / Last 2 yrs / ... / All time** — only shows up under
  Popular, and controls how far back "recently" means.
- **Docs** — only show documentaries.
- **Load 10 more** — show the next 10 down the list.
- **Refresh** — reload the current list.

## What are those little pill badges, like "NETFLIX" or "BBC IPLAYER"?

That's a hint at where you might actually be able to watch the thing, in
the UK. Signal Board asks another service (Watchmode) "hey, where can
someone watch this?" and shows the most well-known option. If nothing shows
up, it just means we don't know of anywhere you can stream it right now.

## Where does the one-sentence description come from?

Another helper called OMDb — think of it as a second little dictionary
Signal Board can look up a quick plot summary in, for just the titles
you're currently looking at (not the whole list — that would be
overkill).

## Does it cost anything to run?

Nope. Everything it uses (IMDb's public data, OMDb, Watchmode) has a free
tier, and this app is only used a handful of times a week, so it never
comes close to hitting any limits.

## Is it "live," like checking in real time?

Half and half:
- The big list of shows/films/ratings is a **snapshot** — it only updates
  when someone deliberately refreshes it (roughly whenever IMDb's data is
  re-downloaded).
- The one-sentence description and the streaming badge *are* fetched live,
  each time you load the page, but only for the ~10 titles actually shown.

## Who is this for?

Just one person, for personal use — deciding what to watch next.
